## Constipation

- **Dupont C, Constant F, Imbert A, Hébert G, Zourabichvili O, Kapel N.** Time to treatment response of a magnesium- and sulphate-rich natural mineral water in functional constipation. *Nutrition*. 2019;65:167–172. [DOI 10.1016/j.nut.2019.02.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nut.2019.02.018), [PMID 31170680](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31170680/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. Double-blind trial, 226 outpatients, 14 days: response 50% versus 29%. Applies to a specific water and protocol, not all sulfate/magnesium-filter results. Provisional moderate evidence for that short-term outcome; generalisation limited.
- **Bothe G, Coh A, Auinger A.** Efficacy and safety of a natural mineral water rich in magnesium and sulphate for bowel function: a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study. *Eur J Nutr*. 2017;56:491–499 (online 2015). [DOI 10.1007/s00394-015-1094-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-015-1094-8), [PMID 26582579](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26582579/), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5334415/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. 106 randomized; 300 mL arms stopped after interim analysis; 75 participants in 500 mL arms. Primary outcome reported as a tendency (one-sided p=.036); secondary bowel/stool outcomes improved. Review the adaptive analysis before stronger language. Producer affiliation present. No universal concentration threshold follows.
- **Naumann J, Sadaghiani C, Alt F, Huber R.** Effects of Sulfate-Rich Mineral Water on Functional Constipation: A Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study. *Forsch Komplementmed*. 2016;23:356–363. [DOI 10.1159/000449436](https://doi.org/10.1159/000449436), [PMID 27924798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27924798/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. 100 in intention-to-treat analysis. The six-week primary outcome was not significant (p=.163); a three-week secondary outcome was (p=.005). Guide wording must include the primary result. Evidence is mixed; do not claim treatment efficacy from sulfate >200 alone.

## Glucose metabolism

- **Naumann J, Biehler D, Lüty T, Sadaghiani C.** Prevention and Therapy of Type 2 Diabetes—What Is the Potential of Daily Water Intake and Its Mineral Nutrients? *Nutrients*. 2017;9:914. [DOI 10.3390/nu9080914](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu9080914), [PMID 28829398](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28829398/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. Review includes 15 mainly small RCTs; heterogeneous results and low evidence. It does not justify a sodium exclusion, magnesium ranking or diabetes water recommendation. Proposed guide level: weak for glycaemic effects.

## Calcium

- **Böhmer H, Müller H, Resch KL.** Calcium supplementation with calcium-rich mineral waters: a systematic review and meta-analysis of its bioavailability. *Osteoporos Int*. 2000;11:938–943. [DOI 10.1007/s001980070032](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001980070032), [PMID 11193246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11193246/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. Four small studies; absorption at least comparable to dairy. Absorption is the endpoint, not fracture prevention. Do not reuse historical intake recommendations as current guidance.
- **Bacciottini L et al.** Calcium bioavailability from a calcium-rich mineral water, with some observations on method. *J Clin Gastroenterol*. 2004;38:761–766. [DOI 10.1097/01.mcg.0000139031.46192.7e](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.mcg.0000139031.46192.7e), [PMID 15365401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15365401/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. 27 healthy subjects; milk comparison in eight. Supports calcium bioavailability, with a small sample. Proposed moderate level for absorption when considered with the review, not a therapeutic claim.
- **Vannucci L et al.** Calcium Intake in Bone Health: A Focus on Calcium-Rich Mineral Waters. *Nutrients*. 2018;10:1930. [DOI 10.3390/nu10121930](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu10121930), [PMID 30563174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30563174/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. Review plus human data; abstract supports examining calcium contribution and availability. Detailed bone outcomes require full-text assessment before any guide sentence goes beyond absorption.

## Kidney stones

- **Siener R, Jahnen A, Hesse A.** Influence of a mineral water rich in calcium, magnesium and bicarbonate on urine composition and the risk of calcium oxalate crystallization. *Eur J Clin Nutr*. 2004. [DOI 10.1038/sj.ejcn.1601778](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ejcn.1601778), [PMID 14749747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14749747/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. Twelve healthy men; short phases and urinary surrogate outcomes. Effects differed by diet. Weak/indirect evidence for patient outcomes; not a recurrence-prevention trial.
- **Coen G et al.** Urinary composition and lithogenic risk in normal subjects following oligomineral versus bicarbonate-alkaline high calcium mineral water intake. *Urol Int*. 2001;67:49–53. [DOI 10.1159/000050944](https://doi.org/10.1159/000050944), [PMID 11464116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11464116/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. Groups of 11 and 10 normal volunteers; changes in urinary chemistry. Do not generalise its conclusion to all stone types, patients or waters. Weak/indirect evidence.
- **Nouvenne A, Ticinesi A, Guerra A, Pinelli S, Allegri F, Guida L, Morelli I, Lippi G, Meschi T.** Effect of oligomineral water with low sodium and calcium content on urinary parameters of lithogenic risk in patients with kidney stones and high urinary calcium excretion: a pilot study. *Internal and Emergency Medicine*, 2015 supplement record. [University of Parma record](https://air.unipr.it/handle/11381/2789271). `verified: false; abstract_read: false`. Candidate identified; record fetch denied (403), exact publication details and abstract not read. Excluded from publishable evidence pending author/university copy. Do not substitute a different Parma study silently.

## Menstrual pain

- **Pattanittum P et al.** Dietary supplements for dysmenorrhoea. *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*. 2016;3:CD002124. [DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD002124.pub2](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002124.pub2), [Cochrane abstract](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD002124_dietary-supplements-pain-during-menstruation). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. This is the updated title of the older herbal/dietary review. Evidence low/very low; safety reporting limited. It does not establish mineral-water efficacy.
- **Parazzini F, Di Martino M, Pellegrino P.** Magnesium in the gynecological practice: a literature review. *Magnes Res*. 2017. [DOI 10.1684/mrh.2017.0419](https://doi.org/10.1684/mrh.2017.0419), [PMID 28392498](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28392498/). `verified: true; abstract_read: true`. Oral magnesium supplementation review; not a bottled-water trial. Water-specific benefit is not established by these reviewed sources. No magnesium-water treatment claim.
